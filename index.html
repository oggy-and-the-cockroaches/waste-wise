Urban Sanitation Bottlenecks

The Cost of Traditional Waste Management

Modern cities face a compounding crisis: citizens struggle with proper source segregation while municipal collection fleets follow blind, static routes that burn excessive fuel and miss overflowing bins.

Segregation Failure

Severe Source Contamination

When recyclable plastics, paper, and metal get mixed with wet organic waste at the household level, recyclable value drops to near zero. Entire truckloads of recoverable material are diverted to already overflowing landfills.

  • Recyclables and non-recyclables get mixed into single unseparated streams.
  • Citizens lack instant, reliable classification to know the correct disposal category.
  • Landfill methane emissions increase while municipal recycling quotas fail.
Logistics Inefficiency

Blind, Static Truck Dispatch

Conventional sanitation trucks travel along fixed geographic routes regardless of whether bins are empty or overflowing. This blind schedule leads to wasted fuel, higher vehicular pollution, and delayed public cleanups.

  • Suboptimal criss-cross routes waste public expenditure and accelerate vehicle wear.
  • Heavy diesel vehicles visit low-fill bins while critical collection hubs overflow.
  • High greenhouse gas emissions ($CO_2$) and unnecessary traffic gridlock.
